Scope: one local macOS ARM64 Jianying 11.6.0 investigation. Raw logs, application binaries, media, drafts, account information and private paths are intentionally excluded.

**The experiments do not establish that all unresponsive states are fixed.** UI redraw pressure, directory access, shared task-queue waiting and external storage interruption have separate evidence and remedies. Waiting for unfinished preparation is not by itself proof of a UI hang.

No patch here adds active-film queue scoping, fixes all directory access, guarantees storage continuity, or demonstrates general opening/rendering/export acceleration. The evidence below distinguishes static inspection, runtime smoke, export/decode, cancellation and controlled timing rather than combining them into a single "fixed" label.

- A native sample contained 1,326 of 1,597 main-thread samples (83%) under `TaskCenterViewModel::updateTaskList(bool)`, with 10.4 GB physical footprint. This identifies sampled UI work, not the unique initiating callback or a vendor-confirmed universal defect.
- The four-byte candidate skipped the delayed completion callback's final task-list refresh. A separate copied, ad-hoc-signed app loaded its patched library. One 300-second compound completed pre-rendering and normalization; a later 905-sample main-thread capture contained zero updater-stack occurrences. Workload and timing differed from the initial sample: this is smoke evidence, not a causal A/B comparison.
- After choosing an accessible local export directory through the native application UI, the copied app exported an approximately 247.97-second H.264/AAC film at 1080×1920, 30 fps. Full native audio/video decoding passed; 12 sampled pictures had low-resolution mean absolute differences of 0–0.860 / 255 against the official-app export. This is not full-frame equivalence or human audio review. Native cancellation returned to the editor and left no aborted final output. The saved project reopened; long-term reliability remains unknown.
- The initial blocked export stack ended in directory enumeration/open. Official and copied export-code regions were identical. A native output-directory change resolved the local symptom; the underlying OS access-stall cause was not established.
- Fifteen observed long compound output groups completed at 141–144-second intervals, mean 142 seconds. Each represented about 627.9 seconds of content. This was historical activity across a working project, not a verified list of tasks required by one cold export. A short final timeline can still reference much longer compounds.
- A short 8 GiB SSD test measured about 797 MB/s sequential read and 446 MB/s average write, with late write windows around 330–360 MB/s. This was approximately 34 seconds of I/O, not a sustained thermal or health test. Results do not generalize to other drives or prove all runtime I/O was fast.
- Pure-audio FFmpeg loudness measurement of two approximately 628-second sources took about 2.05 and 2.06 seconds. It bypassed the application's compound effects, mixing, caching and scheduler. It is not an end-to-end Jianying optimization result.

A controlled same-fixture application-derived-cache-cold ABBA trial is now complete; it showed no throughput improvement (details below). No rendering speedup, safe concurrency configuration key, general audio-only-input patch or universal hardware recommendation is claimed.


## Later full-project stop and bounded canaries

In a later migrated-project run, the user explicitly canceled a full export after more than an hour of waiting. The project was saved and the application quit normally. **No new final film was produced by that run.** Earlier successful fixture exports are separate evidence, not completion of this delivery.

Stable, owned derived-cache outputs were protected locally with hard links and their sizes/mtimes were rechecked unchanged. This is bounded metadata-preservation evidence, not content-equivalence or cache-validity proof. An additional hard link can retain inode data if the application unlinks the original cache pathname; the links share that inode and do not isolate in-place writes or provide an independent disk-failure backup. After the application was closed, the protected links still had unchanged sizes/mtimes, while some owned source-cache pathnames had been removed by the earlier native Home lifecycle. The protected links remained. Home/reopen is therefore not a cost-free queue reset; do not blindly restore outputs whose cache validity is unknown. No private inventory, counts, paths or project identifiers are published.

Two native small transfer attempts used one complex compound: copy followed by normal quit/relaunch into an empty project, and copy followed by native Home into an empty project in the same process. Neither produced pasted content; Edit → Paste was observed disabled. **Status: INCONCLUSIVE.** Whether the Copy command actually succeeded was not positively controlled, and clipboard contents were not diagnosed. A failed attempt describes the missing operational result, not an infeasible or unsupported native migration route. No valid candidate was obtained for speed measurement. First establish a positive control in the same owned fixture: select the intended item, verify Copy is enabled, invoke native Copy, then verify a reversible same-project Paste and undo it before testing cross-project transfer. Investigation continues through design and native validation; a denied runtime debugger attach does not end the overall work.

Practical policy: honor explicit user cancellation; prefer native cancel, Save and normal Quit for stopping a long run, and protect required stable caches. Before any larger run, select the intended current timeline and establish its recursive needed dependencies and valid caches; the exact runtime closure remains unknown and no active-film scope correction is implemented or validated. Limit a new optimization attempt to one representative compound. A three-minute observation boundary means stop expanding the experiment and diagnose/report unresolved scope or progress; it is not a completion-time promise or an automatic render-cancellation rule. Do not use an hour-long wait for the global queue as the default way to validate scope.

The two-job ABBA still showed no speedup, detached-audio output equivalence still failed, and the published candidate's row-attribution gap remains unfixed. These bounded outcomes do not support an "all hangs fixed" claim.

## Cold derived-cache ABBA: two-job scheduling

One independently created native draft contained two distinct approximately 628-second compounds in a single timeline. The complete stopped-app seed (metadata, encrypted timeline data and subdrafts) was restored byte-identically to the same canonical path before each run. Its derived Resources and loudness outputs were absent; the native application generated both output groups anew. Source media were read from the external SSD, while this fixture's physical derived-cache directory was on the internal SSD. Original production projects and their existing caches were preserved. OS page cache was uncontrolled; this is not an OS-cache-cold SSD migration benchmark.

Both copied apps had the same UI patch and ad-hoc signing procedure; only the two-job hooks differed. The native multi-selected loudness checkbox triggered both jobs. Task popup stayed closed during ABBA. Times below are from that action to each finalized video-cache file, and to the last native loudness-result file. Later native UI inspection confirmed both loudness operations completed. File observations were polled every two seconds; final-file mtime supplied the completion timestamp, not the delayed popup redraw.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | UI baseline A1 | 133.629 / 267.102 | 267.928 |
| 2 | Two-job B1 | 267.036 / 267.091 | 268.667 |
| 3 | Two-job B2 | 267.434 / 267.492 | 269.104 |
| 4 | UI baseline A2 | 133.624 / 267.031 | 267.809 |

Baseline mean: 267.869 seconds. Two-job mean: 268.886 seconds, about 1.017 seconds (0.38%) longer. With only two runs per arm and uncontrolled OS cache/background state, this small difference is not evidence of a regression; it is **no measured speedup**. Candidate runs each had 131 observation intervals where two distinct video groups grew together; baseline had only the single handover interval. Video plus alpha counts as one group. Both native loudness-result files were byte-identical across all four runs. Cache-container bytes differed across runs, including between baseline runs; no full picture equivalence follows from cache size or these audio results.

Coarse open-to-visible-editor observations were A: 2.490 / 2.249 seconds and B: 2.718 / 3.094 seconds. This simple existing draft is not a complex project migration/parse benchmark; no opening or parsing acceleration is established.

Separate diagnostic runs, excluded from ABBA timing, showed one versus two native QtConcurrent compound workers with their own render/encode queues. Render queues waited at the same TEVTEncodeUnit input-lock callsite in approximately 66.5% of single-task samples versus 82.2% / 82.0% of two-task samples. Encoder queues spent approximately 98.8% versus 99.5% / 99.2% under VTCompressionSessionEncodeFrame, primarily waiting for CoreMedia semaphores. This supports encode-path backpressure propagating upstream. It does not identify the lock owner, prove a global mutex, or prove hardware/media-engine saturation. GPU-wide utilization snapshots cannot settle those questions. Both diagnostic main-thread profiles had zero task-list-updater occurrences and mostly event waiting.

Recommendation from this fixture: preserve serial scheduling as the practical default. The two-job experiment changes admission, but did not accelerate the lower rendering/encoding pipeline. Prioritize valid cache reuse and reducing equivalent redundant preparation; investigate encoder-session ownership, lock lifetime and intermediate video/alpha format costs before changing concurrency further.

## Later real-project identity audit: physical concurrency, semantic attribution open

A separate read-only audit identified the currently launched experimental app. Its on-disk library hash exactly matched that build's signed-library manifest, and all declared semantic change ranges matched their recorded bytes. This identifies the launched app's on-disk candidate; the audit did not inspect live-memory instruction bytes or replace any binary.

A single compound can have color and alpha encoders, so two encoder queues alone would have been insufficient; the separate compound workers and two growing color families provide the stronger evidence here. No raw filenames, process/thread identifiers, private media or logs are published.

This rules out the explanation that there was only one physical render operation hidden behind two displayed rows in this observation. It does **not** show that the two operations render different useful content, that both are necessary for the active film, or that each visible row is bound to a different native task. Duplicate or stale UI binding could coexist with real concurrent renders. Equal percentages, similar output sizes or distinct filenames cannot settle semantic identity or active-film dependency membership.

The current cap hooks operate on existing queued entries and active-state admission. They do not enqueue or clone tasks, change task/row indexing, or write progress values; they also reject the same already-active task at the inspected admission path. That direct-write boundary does not establish UI correctness under two active jobs: the shared-helper row callbacks need concurrency adaptation, as detailed below. Equivalent graphs from native upstream enqueue/cache invalidation and risks outside the existing serialized-mutation assumption also remain possible. No semantic deduplication or active-film queue filter is implemented.

The unresolved attribution chain is visible row → native task/material/segment → graph and source ranges → output family → active-film dependency. Do not cancel, discard caches or deduplicate jobs solely because two progress bars match. Physical concurrency is consistent with shared rendering/encoding backpressure and with the [controlled ABBA result](#cold-derived-cache-abba-two-job-scheduling): **no measured useful throughput gain**. Keep serial scheduling as the practical default.

## Parallel2 row callback identity gap: not fixed

Later bounded static inspection found a concrete integration gap in **our two-job adaptation**. Precombine progress is emitted by a shared helper. Each running row's callback reads the emitted task's future progress and writes that value into its captured row, without checking whether the emitted task belongs to that row. The ordinary one-active-job scheduling assumption keeps that relationship aligned; allowing two active jobs exposes cross-talk, so both rows can mirror the last emitted progress. The inspected state callback also lacks the identity guard before captured-model state and result-handling side effects. This creates a possible state/result-routing cross-update; an eventual correction must guard both callbacks before their original side effects, not only fix displayed percentages. This is not evidence that Qt cannot run tasks concurrently or that every shipped vendor configuration has this defect.

A native observation showed two precombine rows at 92%; one displayed compound group also contained a normalization row. Such visual grouping does not prove an executable task parent/child relationship. That display is consistent with the static mechanism but does not capture the actual row-to-task mapping. Two separate compound workers and two growing color families still establish physical concurrency; they do not validate the displayed per-row percentage or state, different semantic content, or job necessity.

The cap hooks do not directly write progress or row indices, but they changed the scheduling invariant without adapting these shared-signal consumers. Treat that as an experimental compatibility defect, not as proof of an unrelated upstream cause. **The published candidate contains no identity-filtering fix, and native-runtime validation of that correction is not established.** Per-row progress, and potentially state, must not be used alone for exact task completion or cancellation attribution under the current candidate. Use independently identified physical outputs and native worker/completion evidence; preserve serial scheduling as the practical default. The [ABBA result](#cold-derived-cache-abba-two-job-scheduling) still establishes no measured speedup.

## Native cancellation, export and saved-cache reuse

A separate two-active-task run tested one native loudness task row's X and a later explicit retry. Turning off its loudness checkbox at 76.564 seconds did not stop already-started preparation. Actual row cancellation occurred at 176.421 seconds; the target output was absent at the next poll, 0.826 seconds later. That is an observation interval, not an exact cancellation latency. Its sibling continued through 22 growth observations and finalized video at 221.798 seconds, then its loudness record at 222.649 seconds. A later retry at 257.488 seconds produced a fresh target output: video finalized 133.587 seconds after retry and loudness 134.319 seconds after retry. Native UI subsequently showed both completed at the -23 LUFS target.

The same candidate then natively exported the full two-compound timeline: H.264 1080×1920 at 30 fps, video duration 1255.233333 seconds (37,657 frames), AAC 44.1 kHz stereo, file size 1,915,457,780 bytes. Full video and audio decoding returned zero with no error output. Twelve pictures sampled through the timeline showed the two expected source scenes. Audio was non-silent (mean -28.4 dB, peak -2.9 dB in a decoded volume scan). This is stream/decode and sampled-picture validation, not proof of full picture/audio equivalence or a human listening pass. The original production film was not changed by this experiment.

After saving and normally quitting, the native editor reopened in an observed 2.202 seconds. Both compound loudness states remained completed at -23 LUFS. All four video/alpha cache paths, sizes and nanosecond mtimes stayed unchanged during the observed reopen period. This supports cache retention in this fixture, not a benchmark of complex migration/first parsing or proof that every future reopen hits every cache.

Cancel-all, immediate overlapping retry, failed-render recovery, worker-exit acknowledgement and complete content equivalence remain unverified. The active-entry cap does not prove that canceled renderers have stopped before another starts. Do not infer those gates from the hook harness or the passing single-row scenario.

## Native detached-audio cold gate: fast preparation, equivalence not passed

The same complete normalization-off seed was restored with zero derived cache files. In the serial UI-baseline app, native `分离音频` on the first approximately 627.6-second video compound created an audio clip in under one observed second. Enabling loudness on that audio clip generated only an approximately 15.2 MB audio-combination cache, finalized 4.628 seconds after the checkbox action, and a 17-byte loudness record at 5.252 seconds. Native UI showed -23 LUFS complete. No video/alpha cache appeared in the recorded route. OS page cache was uncontrolled and this is one observation, not an ABBA audio trial. Interactive preparation and eventual video export costs are separate.

For the semantic gate, native lossless WAV exports of the entire 20m55 timeline were compared with normalization OFF in both arms: first compound detached versus both compounds intact. Both outputs were PCM16, 44.1 kHz, two channels and exactly 1255.224308 seconds; both had 60,250,767 decoded frames at the comparator's common 48 kHz. However the relative RMS sample difference was 0.0106621, maximum absolute difference 0.0337854, and diagnostic fitted gain 0.999499. Neither WAV declared channel layout. The strict comparator correctly returned **not equivalent**; no gain/time correction or relaxed threshold was used to force a pass. An intermediate encoding difference is a possible explanation, not a proven cause.

A separate comparison of the original 44.1 kHz PCM localized every differing sample to the detached first compound. From its 627.566667-second boundary onward, all 55,359,404 scalar samples in the untouched second portion were bit-identical. The first portion had relative RMS error 0.01568135 and maximum difference 1,122 PCM16 LSB (0.0342407 full scale); both complete WAVs had 55,355,392 original-rate frames. Whole-file original-rate relative error 0.01066240 agrees with the resampled comparison. Thus comparator resampling does not explain the observed signal change; this is larger than one-LSB rounding. Its encoding or effect-transfer cause is still unproven.

Normalized-output equivalence, trim/speed/fade/effect/mix semantics, human listening, final video correctness and repeated cold audio trials remain separate gates. No transparent audio-input patch is implemented. Do not use this local preparation result as a default project rewrite or a final-export speedup claim.
